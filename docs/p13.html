It was much bigger than that, something on a scale the U.S. has seen only two times in the past half-century.

One was the Newt Gingrich “revolution” in 1994 that put Republicans in control of the House of Representatives for the first time in 40 years.

The other was the election of 1974 that flooded Capitol Hill with “Watergate babies” – new members elected just after the Nixon-era scandals.

In both cases there were high hopes that big changes would take place in the aftermath of remarkable midterm elections. The actual results were more modest than the victors expected.
