NASA’s Swift telescope has burned up, months after a robot sent to save it failed – here’s why it’s so hard to boost a satellite

Wanjiku Chebet Kanjumba, Ph.D. Candidate in Aerospace Engineering, University of Florida · The Conversation · Nov 04

NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory orbits above Earth in this illustration. NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center/Chris Smith (KBRwyle) NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory took its last look at the sky on Oct. 20, 2026, while orbiting about 171 miles (275 kilometers) above Earth. NASA ended the telescope’s mission three days later, because Swift had sunk so low that drag from the thin air there meant its telescopes couldn’t hold steady. The telescope reentered the atmosphere on Nov. 4 above the North Sumatra region of Indonesia and largely burned up, an abrupt ending to two decades spent observing powerful cosmic explosions .
