I study polling failures – this time, pollsters were mostly right, if not precise

W Joseph Campbell, Professor Emeritus of Communication, American University School of Communication · The Conversation · Nov 04

Voters cast their ballots at the Cathedral of St. John the Divine in New York on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. AP Photo/Yuki Iwamura The crucial test for preelection polls is whether they broadly indicate how voters are likely to decide . By that fundamental measure, the 2026 midterm polls largely succeeded in anticipating outcomes on Election Day.

If somewhat tentatively, the polls overall had been signaling important gains for Democrats . And that is what voting produced.

It was, perhaps, polling’s most reassuring performance since accurately anticipating Barack Obama’s presidential election in 2008, an outcome cited in my book, “Lost in a Gallup: Polling Failure in U.S. Presidential Elections.”
