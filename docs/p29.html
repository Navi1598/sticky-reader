Government ownership creates competing pressures that other plants are less subject to. For example, state-owned utilities may be expected to provide affordable electricity, support job growth and generate tax revenue for governments. In countries dependent on payments from companies that extract fossil fuels, utilities may also face political pressure to keep coal and gas facilities operating .

South Africa’s state-owned utility, Eskom, is a prime example. It runs a fleet of aging coal-fired power plants that make it the country’s biggest polluter. But it also provides about 90% of South Africa’s power , and when the country has faced power shortages , it has at times violated emissions standards.
