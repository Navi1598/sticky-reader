Once NASA saw how quickly Swift was falling, the agency had to move fast to try to save the observatory. Space missions typically take years to develop and launch, but in this case, a slow, conventional development program risked arriving too late.

NASA awarded Katalyst a US$30 million contract in September 2025. The company had less than a year to design, build, test and launch LINK . LINK launched on July 3, 2026.

Speed comes with trade-offs. In engineering, a compressed schedule can leave less time for repeated testing, building spare hardware and planning recovery options. Public reports do not say whether that trade-off played any part in LINK’s faults.

NASA has said that even though LINK didn’t succeed, the effort gave the agency experience in quickly building, launching and operating a servicing spacecraft.
