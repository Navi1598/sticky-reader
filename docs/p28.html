At the time, the Florida Key deer population was estimated at only about 800 to 1,000 individuals . Because they were endangered and isolated, primarily on the islands of Big Pine Key and No Name Key , they were monitored more closely than most wildlife.

U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service biologists established quarantine zones. They inspected more than 16,900 animals – dogs, cats, a pig and a raccoon were also found with larvae – and they released 150 million sterile flies across the area. The islands lost 9% to 20% of the total Key Deer population , but the New World screwworm was declared eradicated there on March 23, 2017, less than a year after it was discovered.
