They can carry a lot of energy more efficiently than smaller cars could on smaller roads.

But the high-voltage power is not safe for a home or business to use right away, just as a semitruck at highway speeds shouldn’t try to enter your driveway. So when the power arrives near a group of customers, it needs to take an off-ramp, going through another large transformer that lowers its voltage to levels regular customers can use. Then low-voltage lines act as local city streets, delivering the electricity the short distance from the highway exit to homes and businesses.

Between 2019 and 2025, demand for generator step-up transformers, those that connect power plants to the transmission grid, nearly tripled . And over the same period, demand for transformers to work as off-ramps more than doubled, with costs increasing 80% .

These devices are not mass-produced from automated assembly lines. They are custom-engineered to meet specific needs and demands for their intended destinations. If a specialized transformer fails during an extreme storm or heat wave, there aren’t just replacements waiting in a warehouse somewhere.
