But spot-on precision, while enviable, is not what polls can be expected to produce, considering the many ways error can infiltrate opinion surveys. Given their checkered record in recent national elections , pollsters and media analysts tended to couch their end-of-campaign assessments in measured tentativeness .

For example, Nate Cohn, chief political analyst for The New York Times, wrote just before the election that “it’s reasonable to wonder whether the polls might be overestimating Democratic strength yet again. This is a real possibility. President Trump’s supporters are relatively unlikely to respond to polls, which introduces the ever-present risk of a polling misfire.”

That risk was mostly unrealized, as Republican candidates were swept in a red wipeout .

The 2026 polls tended to confirm the truism in American politics that midterms typically are rough on candidates of the party that holds the presidency .
