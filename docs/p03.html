Outside forces can change an object’s total amount of spin.

Think about spinning on a chair. If you first try spinning with your legs splayed out, you’ll find that you can pull your legs in to spin faster. This phenomenon is explained by the law of conservation of angular momentum . Pulling your legs inward changes your shape, and brings mass – your legs – closer to your center of mass, making you spin faster. Later, the chair slows down and stops. Its spinning parts rub against each other and warm up due to friction , which converts moving energy into heat.

Another example is spinning figure skaters , who spin faster when they pull their arms in. The law of conservation of angular momentum applies to everything, which means it applies to galaxies.
