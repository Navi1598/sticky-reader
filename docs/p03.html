In late July, LINK lost two of its three reaction wheels , which are heavy wheels inside a spacecraft that help it turn without using fuel. That loss, combined with problems with its propulsion system, sent the spacecraft into an uncontrolled spin.

Without working wheels, a spacecraft steers with its thrusters instead, and every burn spends propellant.

Engineers eventually slowed LINK’s rotation by firing its thrusters against the spin . NASA reported in August that they had cut its spin from about nine degrees per second to roughly one and a half degrees per second.
