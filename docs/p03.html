My habit in places like a rocky shore is to become still and let my vision soften. Instead of staring at one point, I allow movement anywhere in the visual field to pull my attention.

At Tongue Point, I was thinking about the demands of life in the intertidal zone , the shore between high and low tide: temperature swings, desiccation and the cost of remaining clamped shut in open air. Then I noticed the soundscape.

At first, the clicking was just coastal noise. I attributed it to small shore crabs moving among the mussels, or hermit crab shells knocking against them. Then one sharp pop pulled my attention to a single acorn barnacle . Its tiny opening was slightly unsealed, and two small plates were moving against its deeply ridged shell.
