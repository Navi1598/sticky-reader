Participants completed two simple hearing tasks. In one task, they detected a tone presented in background noise, which allowed us to assess how the cochlea in the inner ear processes different sound frequencies. In the other task, they judged which of the two tones had a higher pitch, a task that also relies on how the brain interprets sound information.

We found that people with early-life blindness were better than sighted people only in the pitch discrimination task. This means that their improved hearing abilities may selectively originate from changes in how their brains process auditory information and not from how their ears detect sounds.

Importantly, we used a mathematical model to better understand what drives differences in pitch discrimination.
