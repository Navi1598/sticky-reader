Decisions at the U.N. climate conference are taken by consensus, meaning they’re considered adopted when no country formally objects. This act is marked by the banging of a gavel.

At COP28 in Dubai, the conference president “gaveled through” a key decision before representatives of the Alliance of Small Island States had reached the room, meaning they had no chance to object. The lead negotiator for the islands’ group told the meeting that they would have objected, as they considered it too weak to keep warming to the agreed limit of 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 Fahrenheit) within reach.

In 2025, at COP30 in Belém, Brazil, a similar scene played out.
