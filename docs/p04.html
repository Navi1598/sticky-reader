The model showed that the enhanced ability to tell pitches apart in people with early-life blindness happens by reducing internal noise : more consistent and reliable processing of sounds rather than greater selectivity for different sound frequencies.

Surprisingly, we found that musical training seems to similarly reduce internal noise. Sighted musicians also responded to sounds more consistently compared with people who neither experienced early-life blindness nor received extensive musical training.

Why it matters

Because your brain doesn’t know what kind of sensory environment it’ll encounter when you’re born, it’s equipped with a special ability: to tune its responses to parts of the world that matter to it.

This ability helps people see, hear and master new skills as their experiences change, like when children learn to read and when adults
