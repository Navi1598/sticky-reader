Even after engineers brought its spin under control, Katalyst said the spacecraft no longer had enough propellant to capture Swift and raise its orbit safely.

A fuel budget that covers only the planned steps leaves nothing for recovering from a fault. But every extra pound of propellant also makes a spacecraft heavier, and heavier spacecraft cost more to launch and take more fuel to move.

Engineers split a servicer’s propellant among mission tasks before launch. When an early fault forces it to spend propellant regaining control, too little may remain for capture of its target and the long push to a higher orbit.
