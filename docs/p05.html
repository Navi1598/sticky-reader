People will grant friendly agents access to their messages, calendars and accounts, yielding far richer data that the agent’s company can plumb than is usually obtained by a routine chatbot. Cuteness can lower skepticism so people are more likely to grant broad permissions and less likely to read the fine print. Attachment also makes it more difficult for users to leave an agent.

Meta is using the cuteness of Muse Charm to put AI in your pocket.

Too much trust

Making a technology cute can influence what people notice about it and what they overlook. A charming AI assistant may seem more approachable and less threatening, potentially drawing attention away from questions about data collection, privacy, security and surveillance. In one example, Muse has read a user’s text messages and given out another’s home address without the users’ knowledge.

To help people take a more cautious view of cute AI agents, we believe that companies should clearly explain to users what an agent can access, what it can do and who benefits.
