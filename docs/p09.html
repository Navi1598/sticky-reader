Midterms are bad for incumbents – and even worse when energy prices are high

Michael A. Mische, Associate Professor of the Practice of Management and Organization, Marshall School of Business, University of Southern California · Environment + Energy – The Conversation · Nov 04

Voters respond to economic and political messages and experiences. AP Photo/Lindsey Wasson As Democrats and Republicans look at the overwhelming results of the 2026 midterm election, it’s worth remembering what has become a political axiom in America: “ It’s the economy, stupid .” Democratic strategist James Carville famously said that to presidential candidate Bill Clinton in 1992, but he might have said “it’s energy prices” instead. The cost of energy underlies much of the economy , making energy and politics inseparable, especially when gasoline prices rise.

Midterm elections have historically been difficult for incumbent presidents and often shift the balance of power in Washington, D.C.
