Short supplies of crucial equipment stymie changes to US electricity grid

Jasmine Garland, Chancellors Postdoctoral Fellow in Civil, Environmental and Architectural Engineering, University of Colorado Boulder · The Conversation · Nov 04

Large power transformers like this are crucial to the power grid but in short supply. Frank Molter/picture alliance via Getty Images Americans have a lot of plans for the country’s power grid. Some people want to expand renewable energy by building solar and wind farms . Others want to add new coal- or natural gas-fired power plants . There are those who want to provide power to some unknown number of data centers processing artificial intelligence algorithms.

To accomplish any of these goals, the country will need a significant number of pieces of equipment whose design was invented in the late 19th century: large power transformers . These giant metal structures are about the size of a two-story house and weigh as much as 400 tons – roughly equivalent to a fully loaded Boeing 747 . They convert high voltages used to transmit electricity down to voltage levels people can use in their homes and businesses.
