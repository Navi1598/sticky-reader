When an invasive ant disrupted the mutual relationship between a native ant species and an acacia bush, it led more elephants to eat the acacia. The loss of bushes made it harder for lions to ambush zebras, their primary prey, and resulted in a shift toward lions hunting more buffalo, altering the wider ecosystem.

Another fascinating example are sloths and their moths. For a long time, it was a bit of a mystery why sloths would make the slow and dangerous journey to the ground to defecate once a week. As it turns out, sloths host a moth species that lives in their fur. The moths provide nutrients to the algae that grows in the sloth fur, which the sloth then eats. The algae also gives sloths a green tinge that helps camouflage them in the forest.
