Few polling misses

When examined closely, the polls delivered some disagreement in the days before the election. Pollsters offered ranges that, in some races, were sizable.

For example, was Republican Senate candidate Ken Paxton up by 1 percentage point in Texas late in the campaign, or was he down 7 points ? Paxton lost by double digits to Democrat James Talarico, a margin that no pollster anticipated.

Similarly, was long-serving Republican Sen. Susan Collins ahead by 2 points in Maine or was she losing by 9 points ? The outcome was closer to the latter reading , with Democrat Troy Jackson winning by more than six points.

In Minnesota, was Democratic Senate candidate Peggy Flanagan up by 14 points , or by just 2 points ? With most votes counted by the day after the election, Flanagan’s margin of victory was about 10 points .

Such polling gaps in high-profile races offer a reminder to not focus on any single survey but, instead, to consider overall polling averages, which tend to remove or reduce the effects of outliers.
