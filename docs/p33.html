After 1200 CE, archaeological evidence suggests Cahokians ceased or reduced communal feasting and mound building. They also contributed less to public granaries, moving agricultural surplus to private spaces. But it only became harder to live there for some people: Larger houses and status symbols became more prevalent, perhaps indicating a larger gap between the rich and poor.
